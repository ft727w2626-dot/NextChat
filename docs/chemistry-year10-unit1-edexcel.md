# Chemistry Year 10 Unit 1 - Edexcel Pearson GCSE
## Summary of Key Topics and Learning Objectives

---

## **Topic 1: Key Concepts in Chemistry**

### **1.1 Atomic Structure**
- **Atoms, Elements, and Compounds**
  - Understanding that all substances are made of atoms
  - Elements contain only one type of atom
  - Compounds contain two or more elements chemically combined
  - Chemical symbols and formulae representation

- **Atomic Structure**
  - Structure of an atom: nucleus (protons and neutrons) and electron shells
  - Relative charges and masses:
    - Proton: +1 charge, mass 1
    - Neutron: 0 charge, mass 1
    - Electron: -1 charge, mass negligible (1/1836)
  - Atomic number (number of protons)
  - Mass number (protons + neutrons)
  - Isotopes: atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons

- **Electronic Structure**
  - Electron shell arrangement (2, 8, 8 pattern for first 20 elements)
  - Drawing electron configurations
  - Relationship between electronic structure and position in periodic table

### **1.2 The Periodic Table**
- **Development of the Periodic Table**
  - Early attempts to classify elements
  - Mendeleev's periodic table and predictions
  - Modern periodic table arranged by atomic number

- **Structure of the Periodic Table**
  - Groups (vertical columns) - elements with similar properties
  - Periods (horizontal rows)
  - Metals and non-metals
  - Group numbers relate to number of outer electrons

- **Electronic Structure and the Periodic Table**
  - Group number = number of electrons in outer shell
  - Period number = number of electron shells
  - Noble gases (Group 0/8) have full outer shells

### **1.3 Properties of Metals and Non-Metals**
- **Physical Properties**
  - Metals: good conductors, malleable, ductile, high melting points, shiny
  - Non-metals: poor conductors (except graphite), brittle when solid, lower melting points

- **Chemical Properties**
  - Metals form positive ions (cations) by losing electrons
  - Non-metals form negative ions (anions) by gaining electrons
  - Transition metals have variable oxidation states

### **1.4 Chemical Bonding**
- **Ionic Bonding**
  - Transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal
  - Formation of oppositely charged ions
  - Electrostatic attraction between ions
  - Dot and cross diagrams for ionic compounds
  - Properties of ionic compounds:
    - High melting and boiling points
    - Conduct electricity when molten or dissolved
    - Form crystalline structures

- **Covalent Bonding**
  - Sharing of electrons between non-metals
  - Single, double, and triple bonds
  - Dot and cross diagrams for covalent molecules
  - Properties of simple covalent molecules:
    - Low melting and boiling points
    - Do not conduct electricity
    - May be gases, liquids, or solids at room temperature

- **Metallic Bonding**
  - Sea of delocalized electrons
  - Positive metal ions in a lattice
  - Properties explained by structure:
    - Good electrical and thermal conductivity
    - Malleable and ductile
    - High melting points

### **1.5 Types of Substance**
- **Giant Covalent Structures**
  - Diamond: each carbon bonded to 4 others, very hard, high melting point
  - Graphite: layers of carbon, each bonded to 3 others, conducts electricity, slippery
  - Silicon dioxide (silica): similar to diamond structure

- **Polymers**
  - Long chains of repeating units (monomers)
  - Properties depend on structure

- **Nanomaterials**
  - Nanoparticles (1-100 nm)
  - Large surface area to volume ratio
  - Applications in medicine, electronics, cosmetics

## **Topic 2: States of Matter and Mixtures**

### **2.1 States of Matter**
- **Three States**
  - Solid: fixed shape and volume, particles vibrate in fixed positions
  - Liquid: fixed volume, takes shape of container, particles move around each other
  - Gas: no fixed shape or volume, particles move randomly at high speeds

- **Changes of State**
  - Melting, freezing, boiling, condensing, sublimation
  - Energy changes during state changes
  - Limitations of particle model (no forces, particles as spheres)

### **2.2 Pure Substances and Mixtures**
- **Pure Substances**
  - Single element or compound
  - Sharp melting and boiling points
  - Fixed composition

- **Mixtures**
  - Two or more substances not chemically combined
  - Can be separated by physical methods
  - Properties are a mixture of components

### **2.3 Separation Techniques**
- **Filtration**
  - Separating insoluble solids from liquids
  - Using filter paper and funnel

- **Crystallisation**
  - Separating soluble solids from solutions
  - Evaporation of solvent

- **Simple Distillation**
  - Separating liquid from solution
  - Based on different boiling points

- **Fractional Distillation**
  - Separating mixtures of liquids
  - Using fractionating column
  - Application: crude oil separation

- **Chromatography**
  - Separating mixtures of dissolved substances
  - Paper chromatography technique
  - Rf values calculation: distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent
  - Applications in forensics, food testing

## **Topic 3: Quantitative Chemistry**

### **3.1 Relative Formula Mass**
- **Calculating Mr**
  - Sum of relative atomic masses (Ar) of all atoms in formula
  - Using periodic table for Ar values
  - Examples with compounds

### **3.2 The Mole**
- **Avogadro's Constant**
  - One mole = 6.02 × 10²³ particles
  - Mole as a unit of amount of substance

- **Molar Mass**
  - Mass of one mole of substance (g/mol)
  - Numerically equal to relative formula mass

- **Calculations**
  - Number of moles = mass (g) / molar mass (g/mol)
  - Converting between mass and moles

### **3.3 Conservation of Mass**
- **Law of Conservation of Mass**
  - Total mass of reactants = total mass of products
  - No atoms are created or destroyed in chemical reactions
  - Explaining apparent mass changes (gases escaping or entering)

### **3.4 Reacting Masses**
- **Using Balanced Equations**
  - Calculating masses of reactants and products
  - Using mole ratios from equations
  - Step-by-step calculations

### **3.5 Concentration of Solutions**
- **Concentration Calculations**
  - Concentration (g/dm³) = mass of solute (g) / volume of solution (dm³)
  - Converting between cm³ and dm³
  - Calculating mass or volume from concentration

## **Topic 4: Chemical Changes**

### **4.1 Reactivity of Metals**
- **Reactivity Series**
  - Order of metals by reactivity: Potassium, Sodium, Lithium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, (Carbon), Zinc, Iron, (Hydrogen), Copper, Silver, Gold
  - More reactive metals displace less reactive metals from compounds

- **Reactions with Water**
  - Alkali metals react vigorously with water
  - Metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen
  - Less reactive metals react with steam

- **Reactions with Acids**
  - Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen
  - Observing hydrogen gas production
  - Metals below hydrogen don't react with acids

### **4.2 Displacement Reactions**
- **Metal Displacement**
  - More reactive metal displaces less reactive metal from solution
  - Observing color changes and temperature changes
  - Writing word and symbol equations

### **4.3 Extraction of Metals**
- **Ores**
  - Metals found as compounds in Earth's crust
  - Extraction methods depend on reactivity

- **Reduction with Carbon**
  - For metals less reactive than carbon (e.g., iron, zinc, copper)
  - Metal oxide + carbon → metal + carbon dioxide
  - Blast furnace for iron extraction

- **Electrolysis**
  - For metals more reactive than carbon (e.g., aluminium)
  - Decomposition using electricity
  - Expensive process

### **4.4 Oxidation and Reduction**
- **Definitions**
  - Oxidation: gain of oxygen or loss of electrons
  - Reduction: loss of oxygen or gain of electrons
  - OILRIG: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

- **Redox Reactions**
  - Reactions involving both oxidation and reduction
  - Identifying oxidation and reduction in equations

### **4.5 Reactions of Acids**
- **Acids and Bases**
  - Acids produce H⁺ ions in aqueous solution
  - Bases neutralize acids
  - Alkalis are soluble bases that produce OH⁻ ions

- **pH Scale**
  - pH 0-6: acidic
  - pH 7: neutral
  - pH 8-14: alkaline
  - Universal indicator and pH meters

- **Neutralisation Reactions**
  - Acid + base → salt + water
  - H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
  - Acid + metal oxide → salt + water
  - Acid + metal hydroxide → salt + water
  - Acid + metal carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide

- **Making Salts**
  - Naming salts from acids:
    - Hydrochloric acid → chlorides
    - Sulfuric acid → sulfates
    - Nitric acid → nitrates
  - Practical methods for preparing salts

## **Key Skills and Practical Work**

### **Required Practicals**
1. **Making salts** - reacting acids with metals, bases, or carbonates
2. **Electrolysis** - investigating what happens when aqueous solutions are electrolysed
3. **Temperature changes** - investigating variables affecting temperature changes in reacting solutions
4. **Chromatography** - investigating how paper chromatography can be used to separate and identify mixtures

### **Mathematical Skills**
- Calculating relative formula mass
- Using the mole concept
- Calculating concentrations
- Balancing equations
- Calculating Rf values
- Using ratios and proportions

### **Working Scientifically**
- Planning experiments
- Identifying variables (independent, dependent, control)
- Recording observations accurately
- Drawing conclusions from data
- Evaluating methods and suggesting improvements
- Understanding accuracy, precision, and errors

## **Key Equations to Remember**

1. **Number of moles** = mass (g) ÷ molar mass (g/mol)
2. **Concentration** (g/dm³) = mass of solute (g) ÷ volume of solution (dm³)
3. **Rf value** = distance moved by substance ÷ distance moved by solvent
4. **Percentage yield** = (actual yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100

## **Common Exam Command Words**

- **State/Give/Name**: Provide a brief answer without explanation
- **Describe**: Write about what happens or what something looks like
- **Explain**: Give reasons why something happens using scientific knowledge
- **Calculate**: Work out a numerical answer, showing working
- **Compare**: Write about similarities and differences
- **Evaluate**: Judge the importance or success of something, considering evidence

## **Study Tips**

1. **Learn the reactivity series** - create a mnemonic to remember the order
2. **Practice balancing equations** - essential for all calculations
3. **Master mole calculations** - foundation for quantitative chemistry
4. **Understand bonding types** - link structure to properties
5. **Know your practical techniques** - be able to describe methods step-by-step
6. **Practice past papers** - familiarize yourself with exam style questions
7. **Create flashcards** - for key definitions and equations
8. **Draw diagrams** - especially for atomic structure, bonding, and apparatus
