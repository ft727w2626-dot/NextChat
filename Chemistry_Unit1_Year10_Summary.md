# Chemistry Unit 1 - Year 10 Edexcel Pearson Summary

## Topic 1: Key Concepts in Chemistry

### 1.1 States of Matter
- **Three states**: Solid, Liquid, Gas
- **Particle arrangement**:
  - **Solids**: Particles closely packed in regular pattern, vibrate in fixed positions, strong forces of attraction
  - **Liquids**: Particles close together but can move past each other, moderate forces of attraction
  - **Gases**: Particles far apart, move randomly at high speeds, weak forces of attraction

### 1.2 State Changes
- **Melting**: Solid → Liquid (energy absorbed)
- **Freezing**: Liquid → Solid (energy released)
- **Boiling/Evaporation**: Liquid → Gas (energy absorbed)
- **Condensation**: Gas → Liquid (energy released)
- **Sublimation**: Solid → Gas directly
- **Deposition**: Gas → Solid directly

### 1.3 Atoms, Elements, and Compounds
- **Atom**: Smallest particle of an element
- **Element**: Substance made of only one type of atom
- **Compound**: Two or more elements chemically bonded together
- **Mixture**: Two or more substances not chemically bonded

### 1.4 Chemical Equations
- **Word equations**: Reactants → Products
- **Symbol equations**: Must be balanced (same number of each atom on both sides)
- **State symbols**: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) aqueous (dissolved in water)

## Topic 2: Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table

### 2.1 The Atom
- **Nucleus**: Contains protons (+) and neutrons (neutral)
- **Electrons**: Orbit the nucleus in shells (-)
- **Atomic number**: Number of protons (defines the element)
- **Mass number**: Protons + Neutrons
- **Relative charges**: Proton = +1, Neutron = 0, Electron = -1
- **Relative masses**: Proton = 1, Neutron = 1, Electron = 1/1840 (negligible)

### 2.2 Isotopes
- Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons
- Same atomic number, different mass numbers
- Example: Carbon-12 and Carbon-14

### 2.3 Electronic Structure
- Electrons arranged in shells (energy levels)
- **Shell capacity**: 1st shell = 2, 2nd shell = 8, 3rd shell = 8
- Example: Sodium (11) = 2,8,1

### 2.4 Development of the Periodic Table
- **Dmitri Mendeleev**: Arranged elements by atomic mass, left gaps for undiscovered elements
- **Modern periodic table**: Arranged by atomic number

### 2.5 The Modern Periodic Table
- **Groups**: Vertical columns (1-7 and 0/8)
- **Periods**: Horizontal rows
- Elements in same group have similar properties
- Group number = number of electrons in outer shell

### 2.6 Metals and Non-metals
- **Metals**: Left side and center of periodic table
  - Good conductors of heat and electricity
  - Malleable and ductile
  - High melting/boiling points (usually)
  - Form positive ions
- **Non-metals**: Right side of periodic table
  - Poor conductors (insulators)
  - Brittle when solid
  - Lower melting/boiling points
  - Form negative ions or share electrons

### 2.7 Group 0 (Noble Gases)
- Helium, Neon, Argon, Krypton, Xenon, Radon
- **Properties**: Unreactive, full outer shell of electrons, colorless gases
- **Uses**: Helium in balloons, Argon in light bulbs, Neon in signs

### 2.8 Group 1 (Alkali Metals)
- Lithium, Sodium, Potassium, Rubidium, Cesium, Francium
- **Properties**: 
  - Soft metals
  - Low melting points
  - Very reactive (reactivity increases down the group)
  - One electron in outer shell
  - Form +1 ions
- **Reactions with water**: Metal + Water → Metal hydroxide + Hydrogen
- **Reactions with oxygen**: Metal + Oxygen → Metal oxide

### 2.9 Group 7 (Halogens)
- Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine, Astatine
- **Properties**:
  - Non-metals
  - Exist as diatomic molecules (F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂)
  - Reactivity decreases down the group
  - Seven electrons in outer shell
  - Form -1 ions
- **Physical states at room temperature**:
  - Fluorine: pale yellow gas
  - Chlorine: green gas
  - Bromine: red-brown liquid
  - Iodine: grey solid (purple vapor)
- **Displacement reactions**: More reactive halogen displaces less reactive halogen from solution

## Topic 3: Chemical Bonding

### 3.1 Ionic Bonding
- **Formation**: Transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal
- Metal loses electrons → positive ion (cation)
- Non-metal gains electrons → negative ion (anion)
- Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions
- **Properties of ionic compounds**:
  - High melting/boiling points
  - Conduct electricity when molten or dissolved (ions free to move)
  - Often soluble in water
  - Form giant ionic lattice structures

### 3.2 Covalent Bonding
- **Formation**: Sharing of electrons between non-metals
- **Simple molecular structures**:
  - Low melting/boiling points
  - Don't conduct electricity
  - Examples: H₂O, CO₂, CH₄
- **Giant covalent structures**:
  - Very high melting/boiling points
  - Examples: Diamond, Graphite, Silicon dioxide

### 3.3 Metallic Bonding
- **Structure**: Positive metal ions in a "sea" of delocalized electrons
- **Properties**:
  - Good conductors of heat and electricity (delocalized electrons can move)
  - Malleable and ductile (layers can slide)
  - High melting/boiling points (strong metallic bonds)

### 3.4 Diamond and Graphite
- **Diamond**:
  - Each carbon bonded to 4 others
  - Very hard
  - Very high melting point
  - Doesn't conduct electricity
- **Graphite**:
  - Each carbon bonded to 3 others
  - Layers can slide (soft, used as lubricant)
  - High melting point
  - Conducts electricity (delocalized electrons between layers)

### 3.5 Fullerenes and Graphene
- **Fullerenes**: Molecules of carbon atoms with hollow shapes (e.g., C₆₀ buckminsterfullerene)
- **Graphene**: Single layer of graphite, one atom thick
  - Very strong
  - Conducts electricity
  - Potential uses in electronics and composites

## Topic 4: Chemical Calculations

### 4.1 Relative Formula Mass (Mr)
- Sum of relative atomic masses (Ar) of all atoms in a formula
- Example: H₂O = (2 × 1) + 16 = 18

### 4.2 Conservation of Mass
- In a chemical reaction, mass is conserved
- Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products
- If mass appears to change:
  - Gas may have escaped (mass decreases)
  - Gas from air may have reacted (mass increases)

### 4.3 Moles
- **Mole**: Amount of substance containing 6.02 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's constant)
- **Formula**: Moles = Mass (g) ÷ Mr
- **Formula**: Mass (g) = Moles × Mr

### 4.4 Limiting Reactants
- Reactant that is completely used up in a reaction
- Determines the amount of product formed
- Other reactants are in excess

### 4.5 Concentration
- **Formula**: Concentration (g/dm³) = Mass (g) ÷ Volume (dm³)
- **Formula**: Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles ÷ Volume (dm³)
- 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³

## Topic 5: Chemical Changes

### 5.1 Oxidation and Reduction
- **Oxidation**: Gain of oxygen OR loss of electrons
- **Reduction**: Loss of oxygen OR gain of electrons
- **Redox reaction**: Oxidation and reduction occur together
- **OIL RIG**: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

### 5.2 Reactivity Series
- **Order** (most to least reactive):
  - Potassium, Sodium, Lithium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminum, (Carbon), Zinc, Iron, (Hydrogen), Copper, Silver, Gold
- More reactive metals displace less reactive metals from compounds

### 5.3 Metal Extraction
- **Reduction with carbon**: For metals less reactive than carbon (e.g., iron, zinc, copper)
- **Electrolysis**: For metals more reactive than carbon (e.g., aluminum, sodium)
- **Native metals**: Very unreactive metals found as elements (e.g., gold)

### 5.4 Reactions of Acids
- **Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen**
  - Example: Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂
- **Acid + Base → Salt + Water** (Neutralization)
  - Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
- **Acid + Metal carbonate → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide**
  - Example: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

### 5.5 pH Scale
- **pH 0-6**: Acidic (lower = more acidic)
- **pH 7**: Neutral
- **pH 8-14**: Alkaline (higher = more alkaline)
- **Indicators**: Show pH by color change (e.g., litmus, universal indicator, phenolphthalein)

### 5.6 Electrolysis
- **Definition**: Breaking down of ionic compounds using electricity
- **Requirements**: Compound must be molten or dissolved (ions free to move)
- **At cathode** (negative electrode): Positive ions gain electrons (reduction)
- **At anode** (positive electrode): Negative ions lose electrons (oxidation)

## Key Practical Skills

### 1. Making Salts
- **Neutralization**: Acid + Base/Alkali
- **Precipitation**: Mixing two solutions to form insoluble salt
- **Crystallization**: Evaporating solution to form crystals

### 2. Electrolysis Experiments
- Electrolysis of molten compounds
- Electrolysis of aqueous solutions
- Testing for gases produced

### 3. Displacement Reactions
- Metal + Metal salt solution
- Halogen + Halide solution

### 4. Testing for Gases
- **Hydrogen**: Squeaky pop with lit splint
- **Oxygen**: Relights glowing splint
- **Carbon dioxide**: Turns limewater milky/cloudy
- **Chlorine**: Bleaches damp litmus paper

## Important Equations to Remember

1. **Moles = Mass ÷ Mr**
2. **Mass = Moles × Mr**
3. **Concentration (g/dm³) = Mass ÷ Volume**
4. **Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles ÷ Volume**
5. **Percentage yield = (Actual yield ÷ Theoretical yield) × 100**

## Exam Tips

1. **Always show your working** in calculations
2. **Balance equations** carefully - check each element
3. **Use correct state symbols** in equations
4. **Learn the reactivity series** - it comes up frequently
5. **Understand the difference** between ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding
6. **Practice drawing dot and cross diagrams** for bonding
7. **Remember OIL RIG** for oxidation and reduction
8. **Know your group properties** - especially Groups 0, 1, and 7
9. **Understand practical techniques** - you may be asked to describe methods
10. **Read questions carefully** - especially units in calculations

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Confusing mass number with atomic number
- Forgetting to balance equations
- Mixing up oxidation and reduction
- Not converting units (cm³ to dm³)
- Confusing properties of different bond types
- Not showing working in calculations
- Forgetting state symbols
- Mixing up reactivity series order
